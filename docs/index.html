Who decides

Decentralized Open Source Program Office

A non-custodial governance layer that sets policy guidelines, defines maintenance charters, and enforces operator replaceability.

Must not. It never holds direct discretionary custody of treasury funds. dOSPO managers must not act as commercial sales agents.

  • The operator may not set ecosystem roadmaps, project priorities, or development direction unilaterally.
  • The operator may not allocate funds outside governance-approved programs.
  • The operator may not require IP assignment, or take ownership of projects, repositories, or tooling.
  • The operator may not operate any program indefinitely. Programs carry sunset clauses, and the mandate does not continue after charter expiry without re-authorization.

How money goes out

Open Maintenance Framework

A structured maintenance deployment engine: maintainer retainers, vulnerability audits, contributor pathways, and dependency incubation.

Must not. OMF does not collect revenue and does not sign customer contracts. The neutral legal entity invoices and signs. OMF managers must not act as commercial sales agents. Funding supports maintenance capacity; it never purchases technical roadmap authority.

How money comes back

Open Replenishment Framework

A portfolio framework for identifying, validating, collecting, and diversifying recurring sources of value that replenish ecosystem treasuries.

Must not. ORF is not a routing rail. Revenue sources generate new money. Routing rails move money already collected. Allocation engines only set split percentages.

Five revenue families. A, Structural Network Revenue. B, Enterprise Earned Revenue. C, Ecosystem Membership & Certification. D, Voluntary / Incentivized Contributions. E, Capital Income.

Six ratios. Incremental Earned Coverage Ratio (IECR). Structural Protocol Coverage Ratio (SPCR). Portfolio Coverage Ratio (PCR). Stress Coverage Ratio (SCR). Revenue Concentration Ratio (RCR). Mechanism Concentration Ratio (MCR). MCR is disclosed. It is not itself a hard gate.

Eight hard gates. Measurement. Cash Evidence. Net Coverage. Multi-Class Diversity. Concentration Limit. Stress Runway. Liability Coverage. Independent Audit. All eight. Not an additive score. None is marked passed.

Cuts in the series

What is original here

The three-layer split. Coordination, deployment, and replenishment are separate layers. No reviewed ecosystem in the prior-art note runs the full loop under these names with that separation.

Routing is not revenue. A routing rail moves money that was already collected. Drips and Superfluid are rails. They are not a revenue family.

P-scale versus D-scale. External precedent, P0–P5, is how mature a mechanism is where it already operates. Local deployment, D0–D5, is what this ecosystem has shown with its own receipts. A P4 elsewhere can still be D0 here.

The hard gates. Self-sustaining is not a sum of points. The eight names above all have to hold. The lab has marked none of them passed.

Non-powers. Coordination is not control. An operator that can determine outcomes, withhold support without published criteria, shape the agenda, or persist because nobody stopped it has left the design.

Already operating elsewhere

What is a catalog

Cardano POSM, Protocol Guild, Optimism, Polkadot, and ENS are precedents. They show pieces that already run. They are not the contribution.

The catalog is there so a mechanism can be named, rated on the two scales, and kept distinct from a revenue source. Reading a precedent is not the same as having local receipts.

A reader who starts at README.md, whitepapers/orf-v1.0.pdf, whitepapers/ORF_ERRATA.md, and orf/GOVERNANCE_RULES.md can adopt a piece. A reader who starts at docs/PRIOR_ART_AND_COMPETITIVE_ANALYSIS.md or the evaluator sample pass will pick up claims docs/EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md does not support.
