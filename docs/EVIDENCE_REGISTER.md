## 1. Executive Summary & Research Conclusions

This Evidence Register provides an authoritative, audit-ready index of primary governance records, legal entity descriptions, financial disclosures, and protocol specifications across 13 major open-source sustainability precedents.

### Key Research Findings
1. **No Reviewed Ecosystem Implements the Complete Closed Loop Under These Names**: While primary evidence validates virtually every component of the **dOSPO $\rightarrow$ OMF $\rightarrow$ ORF** architecture, no single project implements the complete closed loop under those explicit names with clean functional separation.
2. **Closest Full Closed-Loop Approximation**: **ENS (Ethereum Name Service)**. ENS earns canonical protocol registration revenue, automatically routes proceeds to a non-custodial endowment ($93.39M AUM in April 2026, $8M+ net DeFi returns since inception under EP6.46 IPS), and uses returns to finance operations (covering ~20.3% of 2025 operating expenses).
3. **Best dOSPO Organizational Precedent**: **Polkadot Community Foundation (PCF)**. A neutral Cayman Islands foundation company acting as an "unopinionated" off-chain executor of OpenGov referenda instructions, executing contracts and fiat payroll without holding discretionary policy authority.
4. **Best OMF Web3 Laboratory**: **Cardano POSM (Paid Open Source Model)**. Programs include Maintainer Retainers, Tooling Sustainability, Code for Us, and a $300K Bug Bounty pool (fully utilized by July 23, 2026).
5. **Best Native ORF Protocol Revenue Precedent**: **Optimism Superchain**. Standard OP Chains contribute the greater of 15% net profit or 2.5% gross fees (17,756 ETH cumulative Superchain revenue by June 2025; 26.4M OP committed to 437 grantees; 50% revenue-linked OP buyback approved Jan 2026). Critical negative lesson: **payer concentration and exit risk** (Base 2026 stack transition).

## 4. Mandatory 12-Field Revenue Tracking Rules

To prevent misclassifying grant distributions, token dilution, or temporary spikes as sustainable revenue, every ORF collection report must track twelve mandatory fields:

1. **Gross Cash Received**: Actual USD/stablecoin receipts collected (excludes un-realized token pledges).
2. **Collection & Service Cost**: Itemized sales, legal, tax, and support delivery overhead.
3. **Net Contribution**: Net capital transferred to the Governed Treasury ($R_{\text{net}} = R_{\text{gross}} - C_{\text{ops}}$).
4. **Source Classification**: Protocol, Enterprise, Voluntary, Investment, Issuance, or Routing Rail.
5. **Recurring vs One-Time**: Distinguishes multi-year subscriptions/fees from episodic grants.
6. **Contract Term & Termination**: Captures exit rights and contract duration.
7. **Payer Concentration Ratio ($\text{RCR}$)**: Share of inflows originating from the largest single payer ($\le 0.25$).
8. **Native-Token Exposure**: Correlation factor with native token market price.
9. **Customer Renewal Rate**: Verified percentage of commercial subscribers renewing contracts.
10. **Outstanding Service Liabilities**: Un-fulfilled customer SLA obligations and refund reserves.
11. **Restricted / Unrestricted Status**: Accounting classification for treasury use.
12. **Audit & Provenance Status**: Third-party verification details and transaction hashes.

### Contractual Exit & Concentration Risk Formula
$$\text{Exit Risk Score} = \text{RCR} \times \text{Contract Durability} \times \text{Exit Rights} \times \text{Economic Correlation} \times \text{Enforceability}$$
*Directly derived from Optimism's Base stack transition, where a single large payer's stack migration altered protocol fee routing.*
