## Contradictions USe Me flagged: resolved with quotes

**A. 60% vs "almost two-thirds" quit or considered quitting (2024).** Same question, same base. The measured figure is 60%. The p59 phrase is a loose restatement in the closing narrative and is not supported by any figure in the report.
- S01 p40 (Headline #8 data, n=350, "Have you quit or considered quitting maintaining a project?"): "This year the percentage of maintainers who have either quit or considered quitting their work was 60%, which is consistent with the 58% in 2023 and 59% in 2021." Chart: 22% "Yes, I have quit", 38% "Yes, I have considered quitting", 40% "No".
- S01 p59 (Headline #12 narrative): "Almost two-thirds of maintainers have quit or considered quitting their maintenance work."
- **Use 60% (p40).** Do not use "almost two-thirds".

**B. 60% unpaid vs 47% "not paid" (2024).** Different questions and different bases. Tidelift says so itself, so both figures are valid.
- S01 p4: single-choice self-description, n=437, "Which of the following phrases best describes how you approach your role as an open source maintainer?" gives 60% unpaid hobbyists (16% + 44%).
- S01 p13: "In the earlier question, we forced a single response, versus in this question, we let maintainers choose multiple answers that applied to them. Interestingly, even though the earlier question taught us that 60% of maintainers consider themselves unpaid hobbyists, when prompted with a set of potential income sources, only 47% report that they do not get paid to maintain projects."
- S01 p14, Tidelift's explanation (multi-select, n=421): "Some maintainers who consider themselves unpaid hobbyists probably only receive a nominal amount of income and not enough for them to begin to think of themselves as semi-professional or professional maintainers."
- **Framing:** "60% describe themselves as unpaid hobbyists; 47% report receiving no maintainer income at all."

**C. 52% vs 53% "not compensated" (2023).** Same question and same base (n=253). Both figures are printed in the text of the same page. The chart and the 2024 report support 52%, so 53% is a typo.
- S02 p26, first: "the second most popular answer was 'not financially compensated enough / at all for my work,' also up slightly, from 49% to 52%."
- S02 p26, second: "an increase (53% in 2023 vs. 49% in 2021) in maintainers reporting disliking not being financially compensated enough".
- S02 p25 chart prints no values, only a delta: "+3%" for "#2 Not financially compensated enough/at all for my work". 49% + 3 = 52%.
- S01 p38 (2024 report): "(49% in 2021 and 52% in 2023)".
- **Use 52%.**
- CM §5 says "52% (chart) vs 53% (text) (S02 p25–26)". Correct this: both numbers are in the p26 *text*; the chart shows only "+3%".

**D. Related inconsistency found during the check ("users too demanding").**
- S02 p26 text says "37% of respondents (36% in previous survey)".
- Three sources point the other way:
  - the 2021 report itself (S03 p15): "Users are too demanding and expect too much of me | 37%";
  - the 2023 chart (S02 p25): "-1%";
  - the 2024 chart (S01 p38): 37 (2021) and 36 (2023).
- The 2023 text has the two years swapped. Use 37% (2021), 36% (2023), 39% (2024). CM §5 already notes the disagreement; this settles it.
- Quit-question n captions are also inconsistent: S02 p28 prints "2023: n=349; 2021: n=265", S02 p29 prints "2021: n=349; 2023: n=265", and S01 p40 prints "n=265 (2023)". Treat the 2023 n as UNVERIFIED.

## 59% errata pages (OMF): confirmed

- **2021, S03 p19.** "Based on how many maintainers find their work stressful, thankless, and financially unrewarding, you might not find this next data point surprising: a whopping 59% of maintainers we surveyed have quit or considered quitting maintaining a project." The chart title on the same page reads "59% of maintainers have quit or considered quitting" (all respondents: 26% have quit, 33% have considered). The figure is repeated on p22: "While 59% of maintainers have quit or considered quitting, only a quarter of them actually followed through."
- **2024, S01 p40.** "This year the percentage of maintainers who have either quit or considered quitting their work was 60%, which is consistent with the 58% in 2023 and 59% in 2021." Chart: 22% quit, 38% considered, n=350.
- The full errata draft is in `surveys/errata-draft-omf-59pct.md`.
