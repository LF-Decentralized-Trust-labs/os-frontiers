# dOSPO: Decentralized Open Source Program Office Specification

> **WHO: Governance, Mandate, Policy & Institutional Authority**  
> *Companion to the Open Maintenance Framework (OMF) and the Open Replenishment Framework (ORF)*

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The **Decentralized Open Source Program Office (dOSPO)** resolves the core governance question in decentralized and treasury-funded ecosystems: **Who holds bounded, replaceable, community-mandated authority to coordinate sustainability work?**

While traditional OSPOs exist within centralized corporations or foundations, a dOSPO operates as a neutral, on-chain or community-governed execution layer. It bridges high-level governance (token-holders, DReps, community councils) with operational execution teams and maintainers.

---

## 2. The Governance Triangle

Every dOSPO instance is structured around three non-negotiable governance vertices:

```
                  LEGITIMACY
               (Community Mandate)
                     /   \
                    /     \
                   /       \
                  /         \
          NEUTRALITY <-------> EXECUTION
       (Conflict-Free)       (Accountable)
```

1. **Legitimacy**: Bounded authority derived from time-limited, evidence-based community votes or constitutional charters.
2. **Neutrality**: Complete independence from individual commercial sponsors or vendor lock-in. No single enterprise or delegate can purchase technical authority over core project roadmaps.
3. **Execution**: Operational efficiency, replaceable program operators, transparent reporting, and strict auditability.

---

## 3. Relationship to OMF and ORF

- **dOSPO sets Policy**: Authorizes spending programs in **OMF** and collection instruments in **ORF**.
- **dOSPO is Replaceable**: If the operator of a dOSPO fails to deliver net ecosystem benefit, governance can revoke the mandate and replace the operator without halting core infrastructure operations.

---

## 4. Key Deliverables & Artifacts

- **Community Mandate Charter**: Defines scope, budget limits, operational boundaries, and sunset criteria.
- **Policy Engine**: Governs spending allocations and collection rules.
- **Appeals & Conflict Resolution Mechanism**: Accessible path for dispute resolution.
